views/qt: pixel-art icons as data, coloured from the palette

The goal allows pixel-art icons packaged as icon images, and they are authored as **bitmaps in
`icons.cpp`** rather than as PNGs under a resource file. Three reasons, all of them this
project's rules rather than taste:

  - a PNG is binary: it cannot be reviewed in a diff, cannot be regenerated from the
    repository, and is the one kind of asset nothing here can assert anything about;
  - an icon is eight rows of eight palette indices, which is data -- and the palette work one
    commit ago established that a view's colours belong in a form a test can read;
  - there is no image library in the build, and adding one to draw a floppy disk would be a
    far larger dependency than the feature.

So `icons.hpp` holds the glyphs and the **role** each of their ink characters plays, the roles
are `Palette` fields rather than literals, and `theme.icons_are_well_formed` checks that every
row is eight characters, that every ink index is in range, and that no glyph is blank or
duplicates another's name. `theme.icons_use_palette_ink` rasterises every glyph and requires
every opaque pixel to be one of the palette's own colours -- which is what makes "recolor the
palette and the icons follow" a property rather than a hope, and is exactly the defect an
image file would have reintroduced invisibly.

Five glyphs: a page with a plus (New), a folder (Open), a floppy (Save), an arrow leaving a
box (Export trace), a play triangle (Run). Eight by eight is the smallest grid on which those
are still recognisable and a power of two, so scaling to Qt's icon sizes is exact rather than
interpolated -- which is the whole point of pixel art.

One implementation note that cost a build: `QPixmap` cannot be constructed without a live
`QGuiApplication`, so rasterising into one put the function out of reach of the suite that
checks the glyphs. It renders into a `QImage` and converts, and the test carries its own
`main` with a `QApplication` -- the same arrangement `qp_test_views_qt` uses.

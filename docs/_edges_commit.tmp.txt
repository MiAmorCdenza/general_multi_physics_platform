views/qt: draw edges to their ports, and let a user draw a connection

Two defects, both reported from the screen, and the first one was hiding behind an argument
that made it sound intentional.

**Edges were drawn centre to centre.** `EdgeItem` stored a pair of points taken from two box
centres at rebuild time, and its comment said a straight line was "honest about what this
canvas currently knows". It was honest about a gap that did not need to exist: a connection is
`(node, port number)`, the port number was in the graph all along, and the row a port is drawn
at was already computed in `NodeItem::paint`. What a user saw was a line touching neither of
the ports it joined -- so a **correct** graph looked like a broken one, and the natural next
move is to draw the connection again, which the bus then refuses as a duplicate.

Fixing it meant `NodeItem` holding its port **numbers** rather than only their labels. The
row's y now comes from one expression that `paint` and `port_scene_pos` both call, because two
implementations of "where is the port" would leave a line ending near a port instead of on it.
`EdgeItem` holds its two `PortRef`s and its two items and recomputes both ends on demand, which
also fixes the second half: a node drag used to leave its line behind, because the endpoints
were only ever refreshed by a full rebuild that a drag does not trigger. There is now a drag
handler, and `remember_position` still fires only on a settled move -- an undo entry per
mouse-move is not a feature.

Ports are drawn with their direction: an output is a filled disc, an input is an open ring, and
an edge puts a small dot at its source end. Without that, which way a wire runs is only inferable
by tracing it.

**There was no gesture to make a connection.** A user looking at two boxes had no way to join
them: the demo graph arrived pre-wired and nothing could be rewired, which makes the canvas a
diagram rather than an editor. `mousePressEvent` now starts a gesture when the press lands on a
port **stub** (not the whole box, so dragging a node by its body still moves it), a preview line
follows the cursor, and the release connects -- through `Session::apply`, like every other edit
this canvas makes, so the undo stack and the other panels are in step. A release that lands
nowhere abandons the gesture silently: the user changed their mind, and a message about an edit
they did not make is noise. A refusal is reported, because that sentence is the only place the
reason (a type mismatch, an input already fed, a cycle) appears.

The grab radius is in **device** pixels converted by the current zoom, so the gesture feels the
same at every scale instead of shrinking when the view zooms out.

Two new cases, and the first asserts geometry rather than a count -- the count was already right
when the picture was wrong:

  qt.views.nodegraph.an_edge_ends_on_its_ports
      the line starts on the source node's right edge and ends on the target's left edge, both
      inside a port row; moving a node moves its end and leaves the other one where it was
  qt.views.nodegraph.a_dragged_connection_joins_two_ports
      connecting through the canvas reaches the session, is undoable, and a refused connection
      reports its reason

Screenshot-verified: the line runs from the `Value` disc on the signal node's right edge to the
`Drive` ring on the spring-damper's left edge, and the filled/open distinction makes the
direction visible without tracing.

feat(runtime): add the timeline and the export contract -- L2 complete

core/runtime/trace -- the record of what happened and when. Charter C3 makes the
timeline a platform mechanism rather than a view's private state, and the reason is
worth restating: the obvious implementation is for the plot widget to keep the
samples it drew. It works until a second view needs them -- a table, an export, a
fit -- and the copies then disagree because they were sampled at different moments.
Worse, a *saved* run has no timeline at all, so the numbers in a report cannot be
traced back to the run that produced them.

A sample carries both an index and a time, and the separation is the load-bearing
decision. Simulation time is computed: `t += dt` accumulates floating-point error,
so two samples can share a `t` after rounding. A consumer that identified samples by
time would silently collapse or reorder them and attach values to the wrong
instants. `index` is exact and total; `t` is what a human reads and a fit uses.

Appends are append-only and require non-decreasing time. A trace is evidence, and
the cheapest way to get a wrong answer out of a lab session is to delete the point
that disagreed with the hypothesis. An out-of-order append is *refused rather than
sorted into place*: inserting it would renumber every later sample, so an index
taken before the insert -- a cursor, a cache key, a column position in a saved file
-- would point at a different measurement, with nothing in the record to show it.

core/runtime/io -- the export contract and the format registry. Every format is a
plugin: CSV, TSV, JSON, HDF5, a spreadsheet, a figure, a lab's own templated report
are all defensible, and which is right depends on the course, the institution and
the journal. A platform that embedded CSV would be deciding for everybody. So this
module owns the interface and the registry and writes no file itself.

What the contract refuses to lose is the uncertainty. A CSV of bare numbers is
exactly the artifact this platform exists to replace -- the uncertainty is then
reconstructed by hand in a spreadsheet, or far more often dropped. So
`keeps_uncertainty` is declared, a format that cannot carry it must say so, and a
caller who needs it can refuse the format rather than publish a table that lost its
error bars. The check runs *before* the write and is a separate pure function, so
"this format cannot keep your uncertainties" is answerable in a dialog instead of
discovered in a published table.

Layer whitelist widening, the fourth and last of this phase and again with its
reason in the ALLOWED table: io needs trace, not just store, because what a user
exports is a time series and a series without its sample times is a different
artifact -- a column of numbers whose spacing the reader has to guess.

479/479 with views on; all six gates green. L2 is complete: run, store, trace, io.
